# Learn Coding Concepts with AI

## What you will learn

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- ask for an explanation at the right level;
- move from an explanation to guided practice;
- compare related coding concepts; and
- check whether you actually understand the idea.

## Ask for the explanation you need

A coding concept can be explained in many ways. Tell the assistant what you already know, what is new, and what kind of explanation would help you.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: request a focused explanation</p>
<p class="prompt-text">I understand [known concept or skill], but I am confused about [new concept]. Explain the difference in plain language, then give me a short conceptual example without using a complete code listing. Point out the most common misunderstanding.</p>
</div>

If the explanation is too abstract, ask for a smaller comparison. If it is too simple, ask for the next layer of detail rather than requesting everything at once.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: adjust the level</p>
<p class="prompt-text">That explanation is [too basic / too advanced]. Keep the main idea, but explain it for someone who [specific background]. Use one analogy, one precise definition, and one situation where the concept is useful.</p>
</div>

## Learn through comparison

Many coding questions involve two ideas that appear similar. Ask the assistant to compare them using the same criteria instead of explaining each one separately.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: compare concepts</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Compare [concept A] and [concept B] for purpose, input, output, typical use, limitations, and common mistakes. Give me a rule of thumb for choosing between them, and identify one situation where that rule would fail.</p>
</div>

A comparison should help you make a decision. If it only produces definitions, ask for a scenario and a choice.

## Ask questions instead of copying

An explanation becomes learning when you use it to predict, classify, or justify something. Ask the assistant to question you before revealing the answer.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: practice without immediate answers</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Create three short practice questions about [topic]. Do not show the answers yet. Ask me one question at a time, give feedback on my reasoning, and reveal the answer only after I respond.</p>
</div>

When you make a mistake, ask the assistant to explain the exact misunderstanding. A correct answer without an explanation of your error may not prevent the same mistake later.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: learn from a mistake</p>
<p class="prompt-text">My answer was [answer]. The expected answer is [expected answer]. Explain the smallest misunderstanding that caused the difference. Give me a new question that tests the same idea in a different situation.</p>
</div>

## Ask for boundaries and exceptions

Beginners often learn a rule and then apply it everywhere. Ask when the rule is useful, when it does not apply, and what exception matters most.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: understand limits</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Give me the practical rule for [concept]. Then explain its boundary: when is the rule incomplete, misleading, or unsafe to apply? Give me two contrasting situations and tell me what evidence would distinguish them.</p>
</div>

## Practice: teach the concept back

Choose one concept you recently encountered. Ask for an explanation, answer practice questions, and then explain the concept back in your own words.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: check my understanding</p>
<p class="prompt-text">I will explain [concept] in my own words below. Check whether my explanation is accurate, incomplete, or misleading. Quote the specific part that needs correction, explain why, and ask one follow-up question. Do not rewrite the whole explanation for me.</p>
</div>

## Summary checklist

<div class="checklist-box">
<p class="checklist-label">A productive learning request</p>
<ul>
<li>States what I already know.</li>
<li>Identifies the exact point of confusion.</li>
<li>Requests an explanation at a suitable level.</li>
<li>Includes practice or a prediction task.</li>
<li>Asks about boundaries and common mistakes.</li>
<li>Checks my own explanation rather than relying on recognition.</li>
</ul>
</div>
