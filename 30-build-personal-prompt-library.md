# Build Your Personal Prompt Library

## What you will learn

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- organize prompts by reusable purpose;
- improve prompts from observed results;
- keep templates adaptable rather than rigid; and
- protect private information in saved prompts.

## Collect patterns, not just wording

A useful prompt library records the task pattern, required context, expected output, and verification step. Do not collect impressive wording without understanding why it worked.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: extract a reusable pattern</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Analyze this successful coding prompt. Identify its task, context requirements, constraints, output structure, verification request, and assumptions. Turn those elements into a reusable template with placeholders, without copying private or task-specific details.</p>
</div>

## Organize by workflow

Useful categories include explain, plan, write, modify, debug, review, verify, compare, summarize, and recover. Use names that help you find the prompt when you need it.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: classify a prompt</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Classify this prompt by primary task and secondary tasks. Suggest a concise library name, the information it requires, when not to use it, and one verification step that should accompany it.</p>
</div>

## Keep placeholders meaningful

A placeholder should tell you what information belongs there. Include examples of the kind of value needed in your notes, but do not store secrets or unnecessary private material.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: improve a template</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Review this prompt template for missing context, ambiguous placeholders, unnecessary wording, privacy risks, and missing verification. Suggest a clearer version while preserving its intended workflow.</p>
</div>

## Record results and failure modes

A prompt that works once may fail with a different task, model, or context. Record conditions, useful outcomes, limitations, and failure patterns.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: maintain a prompt record</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Create a maintenance note for this prompt with purpose, required inputs, successful conditions, known failure modes, model or tool assumptions, privacy cautions, and a review date or trigger.</p>
</div>

## Improve from evidence

Change one important part at a time when refining a prompt. Compare results on similar tasks and retain the simplest wording that reliably produces the needed behavior.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: refine a prompt experimentally</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Compare the original prompt with this revision on the same representative task. Identify which change affected clarity, completeness, scope, or verification. Recommend whether to keep the revision and what evidence supports that choice.</p>
</div>

## Practice: curate three prompts

Choose three prompts you have used for different coding tasks. Remove private context, extract templates, and give each a purpose, required inputs, verification step, and failure-mode note. Test one revised template on a non-sensitive task and record whether the revision actually helped.

## Summary checklist

<div class="checklist-box">
<p class="checklist-label">A useful prompt library</p>
<ul>
<li>Are prompts organized by reusable task pattern?</li>
<li>Do templates state required context and output expectations?</li>
<li>Are placeholders meaningful and adaptable?</li>
<li>Are private details and secrets excluded?</li>
<li>Are failure modes and tool assumptions recorded?</li>
<li>Are revisions based on observed results?</li>
</ul>
</div>
