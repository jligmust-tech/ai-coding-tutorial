# The Explain-and-Learn Workflow

## What you will learn

By the end of this chapter, you will be able to:

- turn an explanation into an active learning process;
- ask for examples, questions, and feedback in sequence;
- test your own understanding; and
- record reusable lessons.

## Start with your learning goal

Name the concept and the practical coding situation in which you need it. This prevents a broad explanation from becoming disconnected from your work.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: start the workflow</p>
<p class="prompt-text">I need to learn [concept] so I can [coding task]. I already know [background]. Teach me in stages: explain the core idea, compare it with a related idea, ask me a practice question, and check my explanation. Do not move to the next stage until I respond.</p>
</div>

## Move from explanation to prediction

Recognition is weaker than being able to predict what should happen. Ask a question before seeing the answer.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: practice prediction</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Give me a short situation involving [concept]. Ask me what I expect to happen and why. Do not reveal the answer until I respond. Then assess my reasoning and identify the precise misunderstanding if I am wrong.</p>
</div>

## Teach it back

Explain the idea in your own words and ask for targeted correction rather than a rewritten answer.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: teach it back</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Here is my explanation of [concept]: [explanation]. Mark each statement as accurate, incomplete, or misleading. Correct only what needs correction and ask one question that tests the weakest part.</p>
</div>

## Connect the lesson to work

A concept becomes useful when you can recognize when it applies and when it does not.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: apply the lesson</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Give me three coding situations: one where [concept] is appropriate, one where a related concept is better, and one where neither is needed. Ask me to choose and justify the choice before giving feedback.</p>
</div>

## Record the result

End the workflow with a compact lesson that captures the concept, boundary, mistake, and next practice step.

<div class="prompt-box">
<p class="prompt-label">Prompt: save a learning note</p>
<p class="prompt-text">Create a learning note with the concept, plain-language definition, practical use, boundary, common mistake, my corrected understanding, and one future question. Keep unsupported details out of the note.</p>
</div>

## Practice: run one learning cycle

Choose a coding concept you find confusing. Adapt the five prompts in order, answer the prediction question before reading feedback, and compare your teach-back with the final learning note. Check any disputed technical claim in current documentation.

## Summary checklist

<div class="checklist-box">
<p class="checklist-label">Explain-and-learn workflow</p>
<ul>
<li>Did I state a practical learning goal?</li>
<li>Did I request explanation in stages?</li>
<li>Did I predict before seeing the answer?</li>
<li>Did I explain the concept in my own words?</li>
<li>Did I test when the concept applies?</li>
<li>Did I record the boundary and common mistake?</li>
</ul>
</div>
